This chapter is the first part of the work. It treats the hardware random
number generator of the ESP32 as an integrator meets it: a register that
returns 32-bit words, behind which the manufacturer names two noise
sources and quantifies neither. The generator is run under four operating
conditions in three capture campaigns; its output goes through a
self-validated SP~800-22 battery, through Shannon and spectral metrics
and through the SP~800-90B estimators; a key harvester is built around
it; and a restart test opens the box as far as it can be opened from
outside. Every figure is \meas{} on silicon unless it is said to be
calculated. The chapter ends by stating why none of these measurements
bounds the entropy of the generator, which is the reason for the rest of
the thesis.

\section{The peripheral and what Espressif documents}
\label{sec:esp32_periph}

% docs/esp32_linea_base_90b.md 1: ESP32-D0WD-V3, revision 3.1
The device is the original ESP32, a dual-core Xtensa LX6 system-on-chip;
both chips measured here are ESP32-D0WD-V3, silicon revision 3.1.
According to the Technical Reference
Manual~\cite{espressif_trm_esp32}, every 32-bit word read from
\texttt{RNG\_DATA\_REG} (address 0x3FF75144) is a true random number,
generated from thermal noise and from the mismatch between asynchronous
clocks; the noise comes from the high-speed ADC of the radio, from the SAR
ADC or from both, and enters the generator through an XOR gate. The SAR
ADC brings 2~bits per cycle of the 8~MHz \texttt{RC\_FAST\_CLK}, so the
register should be read at no more than 500~kHz; the high-speed ADC
brings 2~bits per cycle of the 80~MHz APB clock, which allows 5~MHz. With
neither converter enabled, the manual states, pseudo-random numbers are
returned. The software guide~\cite{espressif_idf_random} puts it in
operational terms: the output is true random while Wi-Fi or Bluetooth is
enabled, while the SAR source switched on by
\texttt{bootloader\_random\_enable()} is active --- it must be switched
off before the application uses the ADC, I2S or the radio --- or while
the second-stage bootloader runs, and otherwise it ``should be considered
as pseudo-random only''. Applications read the generator through
\texttt{esp\_random()}, which busy-waits between words so that fresh
noise can enter, or through \texttt{esp\_fill\_random()}, which fills a
buffer with it. For the ESP32 the wait is 16~APB cycles, a ceiling of
5~million words per second (\texttt{hw\_random.c}, unchanged on this
point from ESP-IDF 5.2.1 to 5.5.2, which bracket the 5.3.2 used here); the
15--75~kHz quoted by the guide match the longer waits coded for later
chips.
% hw_random.c: APB_CYCLE_WAIT_NUM = 16 for the ESP32, 1778 (about 45 kHz)
% for the ESP32-S3; checked in ~/esp/v5.5.2 and in the platformio
% framework-espidf 5.2.1 and 4.4.7 trees, and the guide text, on 2026-10-13.

The only validation Espressif publishes is a 2~GB sample, read at 5~MHz
with only the high-speed ADC enabled, that passed the Dieharder suite
3.31.1~\cite{espressif_trm_esp32}; the data are not public, and the
sample covers the favourable condition only. Nothing is published about
the internal state the noise is mixed into, nor any model of the noise or
min-entropy figure, and the literature consulted applies only SP~800-22 or
Dieharder to the generator (Section~\ref{sec:soa_standards}). To an
integrator it is a black box with two documented inputs that can be
switched on and off, and that is how it is measured here.

\section{Experimental design and capture}
\label{sec:esp32_setup}

% firmware/esp32_rng/main/rng_sampler.c, adc_noise.c, main.c;
% firmware/esp32_rng/sdkconfig.defaults; stream time about 25 s per
% condition from docs/esp32_linea_base_90b.md 4
The two documented sources are crossed as two binary factors, which gives
four conditions: A, Wi-Fi and ADC; B, Wi-Fi only; C, ADC only; D,
neither. ``Wi-Fi on'' means the radio started in station mode with
\texttt{esp\_wifi\_start()}, without associating to any network, since
the documentation ties the noise to the RF subsystem being enabled, not
to traffic. ``ADC on'' means a background task that keeps the SAR ADC
converting on channel~0 of ADC1 (GPIO36, floating) as fast as the
one-shot driver allows. That is not the documented SAR source, the
noise-sampling mode of \texttt{bootloader\_random\_enable()}, and
Espressif does not say whether ordinary conversions reach the generator;
none of the measurements below can tell, so ``ADC on'' describes what
the firmware does, not what the generator receives. The conditions run
in the order D, C, B, A from a cold boot: D first, because once the radio
has run a radio-off state is no longer that of a device whose radio has
never been on, and each later step adds one source. The firmware waits
500~ms after each change, and 200~ms more after starting the radio, so
that each sequence belongs to one stationary condition.

Each condition yields 125\,000 bytes, $10^6$ bits, the minimum length
SP~800-22 recommends for its full battery~\cite{bassham_2010_sp80022} and
the minimum sample count of SP~800-90B~\cite{turan_2018_sp80090b}. The
sequence is generated into RAM with \texttt{esp\_fill\_random()} while
the condition holds and only then streamed, with the ADC task stopped,
because ADC activity during long serial bursts was found to corrupt them;
before it, the firmware times the generation of 64~KiB, and it reads the
die temperature before and after. The stream is hexadecimal, in frames
that carry the condition, the repetition, the temperature, the rate and a
CRC-32 computed on the device; the host accepts a sequence only if the
CRC matches and the length is exactly 125\,000 bytes. The console runs at
115\,200~baud, the only rate at which the application output arrived
reliably (about 25~s per condition), with logging at \texttt{ERROR} level
because the Wi-Fi driver's messages broke frames. Before the cycle,
\texttt{app\_main} copies a 4~KiB buffer from the uninitialized
\texttt{.noinit} section, the SRAM pattern of
Section~\ref{sec:esp32_harvester}. The firmware is in
\path{firmware/esp32_rng}, the tools in \path{analysis/esp32_rng} and
\path{analysis/campana_rng.py}.

\section{The SP 800-22 battery}
\label{sec:esp32_sp80022}

% analysis/esp32_rng/nist_sp800_22.py. self_test() rerun on 2026-10-13:
% urandom 15/15, zeros 0/15, alternating 3/15 (frequency, block frequency
% and cumulative sums pass). The module docstring also names biased and
% short-period LFSR inputs, but self_test() does not run them.
The fifteen tests of SP~800-22 Rev.~1a~\cite{bassham_2010_sp80022} were
implemented from the definitions of the standard rather than taken from a
library (\path{analysis/esp32_rng/nist_sp800_22.py}), with the bits taken
most significant first and each test decided at $\alpha = 0.01$. The five
tests that produce several $p$ values --- non-overlapping templates,
serial, cumulative sums, random excursions and its variant --- keep the
minimum, so that a test passes only if all its parts do; the
non-overlapping template test runs the first 8 of the 148 templates of
length~9 that the standard lists. A self-test runs the battery on three
inputs of 125\,000 bytes: data from \texttt{os.urandom} must pass with at
most one failure, an all-zero buffer must fail, and the alternating
pattern \texttt{0x55} must be rejected by the runs or the
approximate-entropy test. It passes: the zeros fail all fifteen tests,
and the alternating pattern fails all but the frequency, block-frequency
and cumulative-sums tests, which a perfectly balanced sequence must pass.

% results/esp32_rng_agosto/nist_summary.csv (15 of 15 in A, B, C, D) and
% nist_results.csv (60 p values, minimum 0.0170)
On the four sequences of the first campaign the battery passes all
fifteen tests in all four conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:esp32_nist}). The
length bounds what that can mean. A perfect source fails at least one of
fifteen tests with probability $1-0.99^{15} \approx 0.14$ (calculated), so
only a failure that reproduces would indicate a defect, and with one
sequence per condition the second-level analysis of the standard --- the
proportion of passing sequences and the uniformity of their $p$ values
--- is not possible. The smallest of the sixty $p$ values, 0.017, belongs
to the template test in condition~A, and it is expected: the minimum of
$k$ uniform $p$ values has a median of $1-2^{-1/k}$, which is 0.083 for
eight templates and 0.038 for the eighteen states of the excursions
variant (calculated), and the template row (0.017 to 0.055) and the
variant row (0.020 to 0.176) sit around those medians. Nothing separates
the conditions.

A pass says that fifteen fixed statistics do not distinguish these
$10^6$ bits from independent, unbiased bits at the 1\,\% level. It does
not say that they are unpredictable: a deterministic generator with a
good output function passes by design, which is why NIST decided in 2022
to revise SP~800-22 and rejected its use for assessing cryptographic
generators~\cite{nist_2022_decision}. Condition~D is the case in point.
The battery passes it, Espressif calls it pseudo-random, and the restart
test of Section~\ref{sec:esp32_90b} shows that without the boot noise it
is a fixed sequence. The battery serves here as a check for gross defects
--- bias, periodicity, short-range correlation --- and finds none.

Each metric has a floor of its own at $10^6$ bits, and the reading starts
there. The plug-in entropy of 125\,000 bytes over 256 values falls short
of 8~bits even for a perfect source, by $\chi^2_{255}/(2N\ln 2)$, that is
$0.00147 \pm 0.00013$~bit; the four measured shortfalls, 0.0012 to
0.0016~bit, are that bias, and the byte entropy and the byte $\chi^2$ are
the same statistic in two units. The entropy per bit restates the monobit
test. The autocorrelation at each lag has a standard deviation of
$1/\sqrt{n} = 0.001$, and the largest of a thousand such values has a
median of 0.0034 for white noise. The flatness of a single unaveraged
periodogram of white noise is not 1 but $e^{-\gamma} \approx 0.561$, with
$\gamma$ Euler's constant, because each bin is exponentially distributed.
In every column the four conditions sit where a perfect source would.
Like SP~800-22, these are averages over the output that a deterministic
sequence can reach, and Shannon entropy is not the quantity a key needs:
key generation counts min-entropy, the negative logarithm of the
probability of the most likely outcome, which is never larger and is the
subject of Section~\ref{sec:esp32_90b}.

% throughput_Bps in the three capture_log.csv: without the ADC task
% 5214513 to 5218249; with it 2766632 to 2771781 (first and second
% campaigns) and 2703184 to 2705416 (third). 64 KiB take 12568 us (D)
% and 12559 us (B).
The rate is timed by the firmware with the 1~$\mu$s system timer over
64~KiB, about 12.6~ms, so its resolution is better than 0.01\,\%
(calculated). In all three campaigns the twelve sequences without the ADC
task were generated at 5.21 to 5.22~MB/s ($10^6$~bytes per second), and
the twelve with it at 2.77~MB/s in the first two campaigns and 2.70~MB/s
in the third; the radio changes the rate by less than 0.1\,\%. The ADC
task halves the rate, for a cause that was not isolated. Set against the
manual, 1.30~million words per second without the task is under the
5~MHz of the radio path, and 0.68 to 0.69~million with it is above the
500~kHz of the SAR path: conditions A and C read 21.6 to 22.2~Mbit/s
while the SAR path injects at most 16~Mbit/s, so even if the conversions
of condition~C reached the generator and every injected bit were fully
random, no more than 0.72 to 0.74~bit of fresh noise could stand behind
each output bit (calculated). The radio path, at 160~Mbit/s, exceeds the
highest read rate, 41.7~Mbit/s, nearly fourfold.

% firmware/esp32_rng/main/temp_monitor.c converts (F - 32)/1.8 from a ROM
% uint8_t; results/esp32_rng_agosto/capture_log.csv holds 52.2 and 52.8
% only (126 and 127 F); temperature_correlation.csv: r = 0.7949,
% p = 0.205; later campaigns: the two capture_log.csv above.
Temperature comes last because its instrument is the coarsest. The
firmware reads the sensor through an undocumented ROM function that
returns an uncalibrated 8-bit integer in degrees Fahrenheit, so one step
is $5/9$~$^\circ$C, 0.556~$^\circ$C. In the first campaign it returned only
52.2 and 52.8~$^\circ$C, which are 126 and 127~$^\circ$F: the whole
temperature range of the campaign is one step of the sensor. The
correlation between byte entropy and temperature over the four sequences,
$r = 0.795$ with $p = 0.21$, was therefore computed against the least
significant bit of the sensor, and it carries no information; no
temperature effect is claimed, in either direction. The two identified
chips later read 49.4 to 51.7 and 61.7 to 64.4~$^\circ$C on the same
uncalibrated scale, so chip and temperature are confounded. A temperature
study needs a calibrated sensor and a controlled sweep, and none was
made.

\section{A multi-source key harvester}
\label{sec:esp32_harvester}

% firmware/esp32_rng/main/entropy_harvester.c, adc_noise.c, sram_puf.c
If the generator cannot be trusted with the radio off, a node that keeps
its radio off needs other sources. This part of the work built a
harvester (\path{firmware/esp32_rng/main/entropy_harvester.c}) that adds
two sources of the same chip whose availability complements that of the
generator: the SRAM power-up pattern, present at every power-up before
any peripheral starts~\cite{holcomb_2009_powerup}, and the least
significant bits of the ADC reading a floating input. These pass through
the corrector of von Neumann~\cite{vonneumann_1951_various}, which
discards the pairs 00 and 11 and maps 01 to 0 and 10 to 1, so that
independent bits come out unbiased at the cost of at least three quarters
of them. The contributions are conditioned with SHA-256 into a 256-bit
pool,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{pool}_0 = \mathrm{SHA256}\bigl(r \,\|\, \mathrm{SHA256}(S) \,\|\,
a \,\|\, \texttt{"TFM0"}\bigr),
\label{eq:esp32_pool}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is 32 bytes of \texttt{esp\_fill\_random()}, $S$ the 4~KiB SRAM
pattern, $a$ 32 bytes of debiased ADC bits and the last field a tag that
keeps this use of the hash apart from the others. Each 32-byte output
hashes the pool with a counter and a fresh read of the generator, and the
pool is then ratcheted forward,
\begin{equation}
o_i = \mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{pool} \,\|\, i \,\|\, r_i), \qquad
\mathrm{pool} \leftarrow \mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{pool} \,\|\, o_i),
\label{eq:esp32_ratchet}
\end{equation}
so that a pool captured after output $i$ does not give the earlier ones;
a reseed hashes fresh generator and ADC data into the pool. Concatenating
before hashing gives the one guarantee that needs no independence between
sources sharing die, supply and temperature:
$H_\infty(X_1 \| X_2 \| X_3) \geq \max_i H_\infty(X_i)$.

% analysis/esp32_rng/harvester_from_sources.py: inputs puf_boot1.bin and
% condD_rep1.bin, ADC field = 32 B of the same RNG stream (stand-in).
% docs/esp32_linea_base_90b.md 1 and 5: the rebuilt firmware stops in the
% seed generation after the first cycle.
% results/esp32_rng_agosto/harvester_validation.csv: 1000 of 1000 unique,
% byte chi2 p = 0.300811, Hamming 128.050 +- 8.027 bits, 7 NIST p values
% 0.147738 to 0.955784.
The harvester was validated offline. The seeds produced on the device
were not captured reliably in the first campaign, and the firmware
rebuilt later stops during seed generation, a fault left uninvestigated
because the seeds take no part in the other measurements. The validation
therefore reproduces the construction in Python
(\path{analysis/esp32_rng/harvester_from_sources.py}) over captured data:
one SRAM pattern; the condition-D sequence as the generator contribution,
the worst condition, on purpose; and 32 bytes of the same sequence
standing in for the ADC field, of which no separate capture exists. Of the
1000 seeds of 32 bytes so derived, all are distinct and their bytes are
uniform ($\chi^2$ $p = 0.30$); the Hamming distance between two seeds,
over 20\,000 random pairs, averages 128.05 of 256 bits (50.02\,\%) with a
standard deviation of 8.03, against 128 and 8 for independent uniform
seeds; and the seven SP~800-22 tests that apply to the 256\,000 bits of
the seed stream pass, with $p$ from 0.148 to 0.956.

% computed from results/esp32_rng_agosto/puf_pc1-4.bin (clean series:
% weights 49.61 to 49.74 %, pairs 6.45 to 6.81 %, mean 6.63 %, stable
% 28804 of 32768 = 87.90 %) and puf_boot1-5.bin (mixed series: weights
% 49.63 to 49.86 %, boot1 against the rest 49.55 to 49.74 %, pairs among
% boot2-5 6.48 to 7.43 %, stable over boot2-5 28719 of 32768 = 87.64 %,
% the figure of docs/plan.md 9); 23.9 % in harvester_validation.csv.
The SRAM source was characterized on its own. Only a removal of power
re-randomizes the cells, since a reset that keeps the supply leaves in
the buffer whatever the previous run wrote, so a clean series of four
patterns was captured with a power cycle before each. The nine patterns
captured have a Hamming weight of 49.6 to 49.9\,\%, and the six distances
of the clean series span 6.45 to 6.81\,\%, with a mean of 6.63\,\%. The
fraction of stable cells, those that read the same value in all four
power-ups, was measured directly: 87.90\,\% in the clean series and
87.64\,\% in the four power-ups of the first, mixed series, so about 4000
of the 32\,768 cells flipped at least once. The first analysis had
inferred about 93\,\% from the mean distance, which counts how often two
power-ups differ, not how many cells ever do; the counted figure is the
one used (Section~\ref{sec:conventions}). The inter-boot distance of
23.9\,\% first computed for the source is not a clean figure: it averages
ten pairs of a series that mixed power cycles with resets that kept the
supply, in which one pattern differs from the other four in 49.6 to
49.7\,\% of its bits, as unrelated data would, and the other six pairs
differ in 6.5 to 7.4\,\%, as power-ups of the same cells do.

% analysis/esp32_rng/mqtt_tls_demo.py, offline mode; outcome as noted with
% the results of the first campaign (secure session).
The validation shows that the construction was implemented as designed,
not that the seeds carry entropy. Every check is passed by construction:
the counter makes every output distinct even if all other inputs were
constant, and SHA-256 turns any distinct inputs into outputs that look
uniform and lie half a word apart. The inputs of this validation make the
point. The SRAM field was one pattern for all 1000 seeds --- the first of
the mixed series, which is not a power-up state --- and the only input
that changed between seeds was condition-D output, which the restart test
shows to be a fixed sequence once the boot noise is removed. The entropy
of a seed is at most $\min(256, H_\infty)$ bits, $H_\infty$ being the
min-entropy of the inputs, and that was measured for none of them. A
weight of 50\,\% does not change this: 88\,\% of the SRAM cells are a
fixed fingerprint, known to anyone who has once read the pattern, and
bounding what the other 12\,\% contribute would take many more power-ups
and a model of the cells. The harvester is an architecture whose output is
at least as strong as its best source, not a measured source of entropy.
An offline demonstration of its intended use
(\path{analysis/esp32_rng/mqtt_tls_demo.py}) expands a seed into a
session key with HKDF-SHA256~\cite{krawczyk_2010_hkdf} and protects a
message with AES-256-GCM, rejecting a replayed and a tampered message; it
exercises the cryptography, and the key inherits the entropy of the seed
without adding any.

\section{The ESP32 reference under SP 800-90B}
\label{sec:esp32_90b}

be closed without the board: the reference side.

The resolution comes first, as everywhere in this work. The MCV estimator
returns $-\log_2 p_u$ with $p_u = \hat p + 2.576\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/N}$,
so even a perfect source cannot score above 0.9963 with $10^6$ bits,
0.9980 with $3.5\times10^6$ or 0.9987 with $8.4\times10^6$. Every one of
the twenty-six captures sits within 0.0035 of that ceiling, and none
implies a bias of ones beyond $2.4\sigma$, which is what twenty-six draws
from an unbiased source look like.

The IID track needs a calibration before its verdicts can be read. Of the
fifty-two runs (twenty-six captures in the bit and byte layouts), four
fail a single test, never the same file in both layouts: one permutation
test in each layout and two $\chi^2$ tests in the byte layout. How often
good data fail these tests at the same length is not the nominal
$10^{-3}$, so it was measured, running the same tool on 1000 files of
125\,000 bytes and 200 files of $10^6$ bits from the operating-system
generator. In the byte layout the permutation battery fails on 1.9\,\% of
good files and the $\chi^2$ pair on 2.4\,\%; in the bit layout
the permutation battery fails on 2.5\,\% and the $\chi^2$ pair on none of the 200. The $\chi^2$ figure was traced to its cause by reimplementing
both tests, which reproduces the tool's fifty-two $p$ values to the sixth
decimal, and running them on 5000 good files. The byte-layout independence
test spreads 62\,500 non-overlapping pairs over 65\,536 cells and merges
cells until each group expects five counts, but at this length its $p$ is
not uniform: the median is 0.175 instead of 0.5, and it falls below
$10^{-3}$ on 1.7\,\% of the files, seventeen times the nominal rate. The
other three $\chi^2$ tests are well calibrated, and the defect disappears
from $10^6$ bytes upwards. Against these rates the ESP32 failures are
unremarkable: one permutation failure in twenty-six byte runs where 0.49
are expected ($P = 0.39$), two $\chi^2$ failures where 0.62 are expected
($P = 0.13$), and one bit-layout permutation failure where 0.65 are expected
($P = 0.48$). On one chip the byte independence test gave
$p < 0.05$ in six captures of twelve, against 2.9 expected; pooling each
chip's captures, where the test is calibrated and more powerful, gives
empirical $p$ values of 0.82 and 0.96 on that chip for byte pairs aligned
with the 32-bit word and offset by one byte, and 0.38 and 0.86 on the
other, so no dependence between neighbouring bytes is present. The rarest
event is the goodness-of-fit failure of the first capture of condition~D
($p = 2.4\times10^{-4}$): that test fails on 0.04\,\% of good files, so
one such failure in twenty-six has a probability near 1\,\%. It did not
reproduce in the five later captures of the same condition ($p = 0.32$ to
$0.96$) nor in E and F, and it is recorded as chance; Espressif's warning
that the generator is not guaranteed random without the radio or the ADC
stands as a warning that these tests do not see. Formally, a single
failure withdraws the IID assumption for that capture; since the figure
carried forward is the non-IID one, nothing below depends on it. Read
with its calibration, the IID track finds the ESP32 output
indistinguishable from a full-entropy binary source at this sample size.
That is the same verdict SP 800-22 gave, reached by a different route,
and it says the same thing: nothing wrong is visible.

The non-IID track says something else, and reading it correctly matters
more than the number. In twenty-five of the twenty-six captures its
minimum is set by one of three estimators --- compression, collision or
longest repeated substring --- at 0.81 to 0.92 bit per bit; in the
remaining one by the lag predictor, at 0.88; the MCV and Markov
estimators never drop below 0.99. Those three estimators are known to be
conservative on nearly uniform data~\cite{zhu_2017_analysis}; the
predictors of Kelsey, McKay and S\"onmez Turan~\cite{kelsey_2015_predictive}
were introduced precisely because the earlier estimators of the standard
misjudge sources of that kind. The repetitions show that this is
estimator behaviour and not a property of the conditions. The compression
estimate has a standard deviation of 0.02 to 0.04 \emph{within} a
condition, as large as the 0.03 that separates the four condition means,
and a one-way analysis of variance on each of the eleven estimators finds
no difference between conditions: the smallest $p$ is 0.083, for the
MultiMCW predictor, above the uncorrected 0.05 and far above the 0.0045
that eleven tests demand. The same holds between chips. Grouping the
conditions, which do not differ, the twelve captures of each chip are
separated by no estimator at the corrected level; the smallest $p$, 0.047
for MCV, corresponds to a difference of 0.0007 bit per bit next to the
resolution ceiling, and one value below 0.05 among eleven tests is what
chance provides. The non-IID minimum is 0.852 on one chip and 0.847 on the
other: with two devices,
the reference does not depend on which one is measured, at the resolution
of these estimators. The largest capture adds a
second illustration of the resolution effect: in the byte layout its
limiting estimator is the $t$-tuple test at $t = 2$, which returns 7.39
bits per byte because $10^6$ bytes hold too few of the 65\,536 possible
pairs for the most frequent one to be representative, exactly as the MCV
ceiling does at $t = 1$. No difference between Wi-Fi on, ADC on and
neither is resolvable by any estimator with six repetitions, and none
is claimed.

The restart test of Section~3.1.4 of the standard answers what the
statistics above cannot: if a generator has no noise of its own, its
output after a cold restart must repeat. A dedicated firmware read
1024~bytes with \texttt{esp\_fill\_random} as the first action of the
application, with the radio and the ADC off (condition~D), and the chip of
MAC a0:dd:6c:0e:fb:4c was reset through its enable pin 1000 times; the
boot ROM reported a power-on reset every time. Two variants share the
application and differ only in the second-stage bootloader: the stock
one, which feeds SAR ADC noise into the generator for about 126~ms while
it runs, and the same bootloader with that call diverted to an empty
function at link time, which the disassembly confirms. Three further
variants read a second time after starting the Wi-Fi as in conditions A
and B, without associating to any network, and waiting 200~ms, with each
of the two bootloaders; the third is a control that waits the same time
without touching the radio, so that the read falls on the same scheduler
tick. Table~\ref{tab:esp32_restart} gives the outcome.

With the stock bootloader the generator passes in both layouts, and the
1000 outputs differ from one another exactly as random data would: the
mean Hamming distance between two restarts is 4096.0 bits of 8192,
against $4096 \pm 45$ expected. Without the boot noise it fails
decisively, and the data show why. Each restart is a window on one fixed
sequence, read from a slightly different starting point: wherever the
beginning of one restart's output appears inside another's, displaced by
a few 32-bit words, the whole overlap matches word for word, 5424 times
without a single difference. The only thing that changes between restarts
is the instant at which the application starts reading, which varies by a
few thousand clock cycles from one boot to the next. That is worth between
6.5 bits per restart, the bound of the standard from the most repeated
output, and 10.8 bits if every starting point were equally likely, out of
8192 bits read. With the radio and the ADC off the generator has no noise
of its own, and Espressif's statement that its output is then
pseudo-random only~\cite{espressif_idf_random} is confirmed directly,
which no statistical test on a continuous stream could do. Two
consequences follow. The condition~D captures of
Table~\ref{tab:esp32_90b} are the output of a deterministic generator
seeded during boot: the estimators credit them with 0.85 bit per bit, as
much as with the radio on, while their physical entropy is at most what
the bootloader injected, which those estimators do not measure. And an
application that derives a key right after boot with the radio off
depends entirely on the stock bootloader's noise; without it, the key
could be guessed in a few thousand attempts, the cold-boot problem
described by Blanco-Romero et al.~\cite{blancoromero_2026_entropy}.

The radio rows answer the other half of Espressif's statement. With the
boot noise removed, starting the Wi-Fi is enough for the test to pass:
the 1000 outputs are all distinct, no window is shared, and the 289 pairs
of restarts that read identical values before the radio was started read
289 different values after it. Timing alone does not explain this. The
control waits the same time and reads on the same scheduler tick, with a
read instant that varies as much (3824 against 5260 clock cycles among
the restarts that share the tick), and it repeats exactly as at boot:
760 distinct outputs, 5632 identical overlaps, about 1700 states. With so
few states, the 797 restarts that share a tick in the radio variant would
show about 180 repeats by timing alone; they show none. Without the
radio the generator receives no noise in its first 350~ms of operation;
with the radio it does. What the test does not give is how much entropy
per bit the radio delivers: the validated 0.83 bit per bit is the
estimators' figure, with their usual bias, not a measurement of the
radio noise. A restart test of the ESP32 generator has not been found in
the literature consulted.

\section{What a black box can and cannot tell}
\label{sec:esp32_lessons}

Every statistical instrument applied to the ESP32 generator gives the
same answer. SP~800-22 passes all fifteen tests in all four conditions;
the Shannon, autocorrelation, spectral and compression metrics sit where
a perfect source would at this length; the IID track of SP~800-90B, read
with its calibration, finds the output indistinguishable from a
full-entropy binary source; and the non-IID track credits 0.81 to
0.92~bit per bit, with no difference between conditions or between chips
that any estimator resolves. None of these figures distinguishes
condition~D, in which Espressif says the generator is pseudo-random, from
condition~A, in which both documented sources are active.

The restart test is the one measurement that does, because it asks a
physical question rather than a statistical one: does the output repeat
when the generator starts again from the same state? With the boot noise
removed and the radio off it repeats, word for word; with the radio on it
does not. The generator has no noise of its own with the radio and the
ADC off, and the radio does inject noise into it. The SP~800-90B figures
of condition~D are therefore the estimators' verdict on the deterministic
continuation of a boot seed: a statistical figure, which cannot be
credited as physical entropy. The same holds, less visibly, for the other
conditions. The restart test shows that the radio injects noise, not how
much; for conditions A and C it is not even established that the ADC
conversions reach the generator, and by the manual's own rates the SAR
path could not supply one bit of fresh noise per output bit at the rate
the generator was read.

What is missing is not a better test but a different kind of knowledge:
a model of the source --- which physical process produces the randomness,
and with which parameters --- and a measurement of those parameters that
bounds the min-entropy from below. Neither can be obtained from outside
the ESP32: its noise sources, internal state and post-processing are
undocumented, and the SP~800-90B estimators were designed for raw noise
samples, not for the output of a block that may already be conditioned.
The second part of this work therefore builds the generator instead of
measuring someone else's. Chapter~\ref{ch:ch3_entropy} starts from the
physics of a ring oscillator, extracts its jitter and turns it, through a
validated model, into a lower bound on min-entropy; the chapters that
follow build and verify the engine around that source. The ESP32 stays
in the work in two other roles: its non-IID figure is the reference of
the comparison in Section~\ref{sec:esp32_ref}, and its cryptographic
library is the independent implementation against which the engine is
verified.
