\chapter{The Two FPGA Boards: Basys~2 and Basys~3}
\label{app:fpga}

The entropy source was taken to two \ac{FPGA} boards. The board available
first (14--16 November 2026) was a Digilent Basys~2 with a Spartan-3E
XC3S100E (90\,nm); the design had been prepared for a Basys~3 with an
Artix-7 XC7A35T (28\,nm), which arrived on 17 November and on which almost
everything was repeated. The results of this work are those of the
Basys~3. The Basys~2 results are kept as a preliminary run: what they
taught was applied to the Basys~3, and one experiment (the sampling
flip-flop with and without clock enable) exists only there. In this
appendix the two ring oscillators are named as in the \ac{RTL}: RO1 is the
sampled ring and RO2 the sampling ring, and the divider is
$K_D = 2^{n}$ periods of RO2 (register \texttt{CFG\_KD} $= n$). $q_2$ is, as in
Chapter~\ref{ch:ch3_entropy}, the variance of the relative phase between
the rings accumulated per RO2 period, in RO1 cycles squared. All values are \meas{} on the board unless
marked otherwise.

\paragraph{What is common.} On two families and two process nodes the
picture is the same. The $c(M)$ fit gives a relative phase noise of
$3\times10^{-6}$ to $5\times10^{-5}$ per RO2 period
(Table~\ref{tab:app_fpga_res}), and the \ac{ERO} model leaves
residuals of several to tens of times the binomial noise: part of the
phase is a deterministic modulation, not jitter. $q_2$ depends on the logic
attached to the rings and on the implementation, in the same direction on
both boards (without the frequency meter $q_2$ is larger). SP~800-90B
saturates at 0.82--0.88 with $10^6$ bits and separates neither the
\ac{FPGA} from the ESP32 and from ideal bits, nor, on the Basys~3, a
divider with $H_\infty = 0.47$ from one with 0.9996. The bias is
0.2--1.9\,\% and its sign changes between implementations on both boards.
The health tests do not see the low-jitter regime, so only the minimum
divider protects it. The restart test passes on both.

\paragraph{What changed the divider.} Not the board but the method. The
$c(M)$ fit counts the modulation as jitter and overestimates $q_2$; the
spectral method, available only on the Basys~3 because it needs the
capture buffer at small dividers, separates the white floor, 13--24 times
below the fit in the same variant. With it the \ac{FPGA} divider went from
$n \ge 16$ to $n = 19$. The Basys~2 bound of $n \ge 16$ can be neither
confirmed nor ruled out. The same $n$ is not the same time on both boards:
$n = 16$ at 143.8\,MHz is 456\,$\mu$s per bit, and $n = 19$ at 205\,MHz
is 2.56\,ms.

\paragraph{What the Basys~2 alone shows.} Only there was the sampling
flip-flop compared with and without clock enable, with the rings and the
flip-flop at the same sites: with it, $\rho_1 \approx 0.005$; without it,
nothing. This is why the engine samples without clock enable by default
(Chapter~\ref{ch:ch4_engine}). The Basys~3 has no variant with clock enable,
so the cause rests on one family; the absence of correlation without it is
confirmed on both, in streams of consecutive bits.

\paragraph{Lessons carried over.} The Basys~2 showed that a ring can vanish
in the implementation (the mapper collapsed the inverter chain), that
placed rings run faster than the counter of the \ac{ERO} closes, that the
multiplexed display modulates the rings, and that repeatability has to be
measured before a difference is read. On the Basys~3 the build script lists
every ring cell after routing and stops if a ring is missing, the stage
count and the RO2 timing constraint follow the measured frequency, the
campaigns use the quiet wrapper, and the captures are repeated: three on
the first bitstream, without fixed placement, two on those with fixed
placement, from which the values used here come, and one on the final
bitstream.

\paragraph{90\,nm against 28\,nm.} Placed, the Artix-7 rings are about
2--2.3 times faster per stage. The phase noise is of the same order on
both boards, but with different frequencies, dividers, fit ranges and
wrappers, and with a model that does not describe its shape on either; no
conclusion is drawn about which node has more jitter. The only figure that
aims to be thermal, the white floor of the Basys~3
($\sqrt{q_2}\,T_2 \approx 4$--$5$\,ps), is the relative jitter of the two
rings from an estimator that errs upwards, and is of the order of the
2--4\,ps per period reported for one ring oscillator in
28\,nm~\cite{petura_2016_survey}.
